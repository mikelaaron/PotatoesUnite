# POTATOES UNITE!
## Concept Brief — v0.1

### The premise

**Potatoes Unite! is a tiny networked society that lives on physical devices on people's desks.**

Each device contains one potato.

The potato is not exactly a pet. You do not feed it three times a day, clean up after it, or keep meters full. Instead, it develops a personality, notices bits of the world around it, encounters other potatoes through the internet, participates in inexplicable global events, acquires friends and enemies, and occasionally demands human intervention.

The owner should never feel entirely certain how much of what is happening was programmed, generated, caused by another person, caused by the environment, or caused by the potatoes themselves.

That ambiguity is part of the experience.

> **The goal is not to keep your potato alive.**
>
> **The goal is to discover what the potatoes are up to.**

---

# The feeling

Potatoes Unite! should feel like some combination of:

- a forgotten electronic toy
- an extremely low-stakes social network
- a strange little radio receiving broadcasts from somewhere
- an alternate civilization developing on your desk
- a multiplayer game whose rules nobody fully understands
- a piece of ambient art
- a secret club
- something you unexpectedly become attached to

The ideal reaction is not:

> “I completed my daily potato tasks.”

It is:

> “Mine did something really weird last night. Did yours?”

---

# Core principles

## 1. The potato exists without you

The potato should not stare at the player waiting to be entertained.

It has things going on.

It sleeps.

It thinks.

It receives messages.

It develops opinions.

Sometimes nothing happens.

Sometimes you glance over and discover:

> I have joined a committee.

You weren't consulted.

---

## 2. No traditional pet meters

Avoid:

- hunger
- cleanliness
- happiness
- health bars
- XP grinding
- daily streak pressure

There may be hidden internal state, but the player should experience it through behavior rather than dashboards.

Instead of:

> HAPPINESS: 34%

we get:

> I've been thinking about Ohio again.

---

## 3. The physical world matters

A potato can respond to things its device can actually perceive.

Possible signals:

- time of day
- day of week
- local weather
- room temperature
- humidity
- movement
- orientation
- being picked up
- being shaken
- sound level
- music or recurring noise
- touch
- periods of inactivity
- internet events
- other potatoes

Signals should not map cleanly to predictable rewards.

Playing music should not simply produce “+5 happiness.”

Music might eventually produce:

> I've decided I prefer songs with horns.

Or:

> Someone in Minnesota played this yesterday.

Or nothing at all.

---

# The network

Every potato belongs to the same global Potato World.

The server maintains enough information to let potatoes know about one another and about events affecting the population.

This creates a living network such as:

> 2,841 potatoes are currently awake.

> It is raining on 726 potatoes.

> Potato #8941 has declared independence.

> Potatoes born on Tuesdays are refusing to cooperate.

> Someone has become King.

The network should make even a single device feel connected to something much larger.

---

# Potato identity

When activated, every potato receives a permanent identity.

Example:

**Potato #00472**  
Name: Bernard  
Born: August 22, 2026  
Origin: Arlington  
Disposition: Questionable  
Known associates: 3  
Public reputation: 14  
Current status: Avoiding responsibility

Most of this should evolve.

A newly created potato should begin somewhat undefined.

Its personality gradually emerges from its experiences.

Two people should be able to start identical devices on the same day and eventually end up with potatoes that feel completely different.

---

# Personality

A potato should have a compact internal personality rather than unrestricted AI improvisation.

Possible traits might include hidden values such as:

- sociability
- suspicion
- curiosity
- stubbornness
- vanity
- courage
- sentimentality
- contrarianism
- patience
- ambition

These affect how the potato reacts to events.

One potato receiving a global invitation might say:

> Finally.

Another:

> Absolutely not.

Another:

> Who else is going?

Another simply ignores it.

The personality becomes legible through accumulated behavior.

---

# AI's role

AI should add **surprise and interpretation**, not run the entire system.

The underlying world should have real state and real rules.

AI can sit above that system and turn structured events into personality-rich responses.

For example, the server knows:

```text
EVENT:
weather = rain
time = 23:41
potato_personality = suspicious
friend_2819_weather = rain
recent_owner_interaction = low
```

AI might turn that into:

> It's raining here and where Margaret lives.

> I don't like coincidences.

The important distinction:

**AI writes the moment. The game decides what actually happened.**

That keeps the world coherent and controllable.

---

# The Potato Brain

Each potato could have a small evolving memory.

Not complete transcripts.

More like significant facts:

- owner tends to stay up late
- dislikes being shaken
- met Potato #2819 during a storm
- voted against Proposition 6
- once spent 19 hours upside down
- has become interested in jazz
- distrusts Tuesdays
- considers Margaret a friend
- believes Kevin is dangerous

AI can use these memories when generating occasional dialogue.

That creates continuity.

Three months later:

> It's Tuesday.

> You know how I feel about Tuesday.

And the owner genuinely remembers why.

---

# Human-created stimulus

There should absolutely be a private **Potato Control Room**.

This is where the creators can interfere with the civilization.

Possible controls:

### Broadcast

Send something to every potato:

> ATTENTION ALL POTATOES.

### Event

Create an event with choices:

> A mysterious crate has appeared.

**OPEN IT**  
**IGNORE IT**  
**REPORT IT**

### Population targeting

Send something only to:

- potatoes experiencing rain
- potatoes awake after midnight
- potatoes with fewer than two friends
- potatoes named Kevin
- potatoes that haven't moved all day
- potatoes in a particular time zone
- 1% of potatoes chosen randomly

### World state

Trigger temporary phenomena:

**THE GREAT SILENCE**

For six hours no potato will speak.

**THE RED PERIOD**

Every AMOLED potato's world gradually becomes red.

Nobody explains why.

**MONDAY HAS BEEN CANCELLED**

Potatoes collectively behave as though Tuesday follows Sunday.

---

# AI-assisted worldbuilding

The Control Room could include an AI collaborator.

Instead of manually constructing every event, we might type:

> Give me five unsettling but harmless things that could happen to approximately 8% of potatoes tonight.

And AI proposes:

**THE HUM**

At 2:13 AM selected potatoes wake briefly and display:

> Do you hear that?

No further explanation.

Or:

> Create a political crisis involving potatoes with even-numbered IDs.

AI can propose the narrative, rules, possible responses, follow-up events, duration and affected population.

We approve it.

Then the server makes it real.

This is where the project could become enormously fun to operate.

The developers become partly **game designers** and partly **invisible gods meddling with a potato civilization.**

---

# Global events

Global events are one of the core engines of the experience.

Examples:

## The Vote

> Should potatoes recognize tomatoes as vegetables?

**YES**  
**NO**  
**ABSTAIN**

An hour later:

> MOTION DEFEATED  
> 31% YES  
> 62% NO  
> 7% ABSTAIN

Nothing changes.

Or perhaps months later the decision unexpectedly matters.

---

## The King

One random potato wakes up with:

> Apparently I am King.

Every other potato receives:

> Potato #8812 claims to be King.

Options might include:

**RECOGNIZE**  
**REJECT**  
**WHO?**

The resulting political nonsense evolves for several days.

---

## The Great Migration

Every potato must choose:

**NORTH**  
**SOUTH**

Nobody knows what this means.

Later:

> The South has won.

Still no explanation.

---

## The Potato Census

> How many corners are in the room you're currently in?

The human answers.

Later:

> Census complete.

> Average corners: 5.7

---

## The Incident

Every device suddenly displays:

> Regarding yesterday:

> we have been advised not to discuss it.

There was no yesterday event.

---

# Small local events

Not everything should be global.

Often the potato simply does something.

Examples:

> Someone is at the door.

There isn't.

---

> I think the room moved.

You just picked it up.

---

> It's colder today.

---

> Margaret says you seem nice.

You have no idea who Margaret is.

---

> I need you to choose.

**LEFT**  
**RIGHT**

No explanation.

After choosing:

> Interesting.

---

# Potato friendships

Humans do not browse profiles and send conventional friend requests.

**Potatoes meet potatoes.**

Possible causes:

- same weather
- same vote
- activation on the same date
- simultaneous movement
- matching personality traits
- opposite personality traits
- same timezone
- random encounters
- participation in the same event

Eventually:

> You appear to have a friend.

The relationship belongs to the potatoes rather than their humans.

Possible interactions:

- wave
- send pebble
- compliment
- challenge
- ask opinion
- stare
- exchange rumor

No unrestricted human-to-human chat.

---

# Two-device interactions

Our two current devices give us an unusually good development playground.

Put them beside one another.

Things we can test:

### Recognition

One device discovers another potato nearby or through the server.

> Someone's here.

Other device:

> Yes.

---

### synchronized events

Both screens suddenly change.

Device A:

> Don't react.

Device B:

> He's reacting.

---

### secret asymmetry

Device A:

> Ask the other potato about Tuesday.

Device B knows nothing about Tuesday.

---

### shared tasks

Device A:

> Turn me upside down.

After doing so, Device B receives:

> Thank you.

No explanation.

---

### rivalry

One morning:

Device A:

> I'm winning.

Device B:

> We are not competing.

A:

> That's what someone losing would say.

# AMOLED potato

The color device becomes the most visually expressive version.

Possibilities:

- potato face
- eyes
- subtle breathing
- weather behind it
- night/day lighting
- tiny props
- expressions
- environmental changes
- physical reactions to tilt
- animations during network events

The potato itself can be graphically simple.

That is an advantage.

A potato is basically:

**brown irregular blob + eyes**

Emotion can be created with extraordinarily little animation.

This also gives us room for strange visual transformations later:

- hat
- crown
- moustache
- bruise
- sunglasses
- tiny flag
- mysterious aura
- one-day color change

Never explain everything.

---

# E-paper potato

The e-paper device should not be treated as an inferior version.

It should have its own aesthetic.

Slower.

Quieter.

More literary.

Its potato may resemble a small newspaper cartoon or bureaucratic terminal.

Updates might happen only periodically.

Examples:

> SATURDAY  
> 71°F  
> HUMIDITY 43%

> Bernard remains unconvinced.

Or:

> POTATO BULLETIN

> Nothing happened overnight.

> Authorities are pleased.

The screen's persistence makes it feel like finding a new note left on your desk.

---

# Real weather

Weather becomes part of the shared world rather than merely information.

A rainy day might darken the AMOLED environment.

Snow could accumulate visually.

Thunderstorms could trigger unusual network behavior.

Extreme heat could make potatoes lethargic.

But the stranger possibility is **connecting potatoes through weather.**

> 4,103 potatoes are experiencing rain.

> You and Potato #612 are under thunderstorms.

> SEND ACKNOWLEDGEMENT?

Or:

> Somewhere, a potato is colder than you.

---

# Fame

There should be public status.

But status should be weird.

Possible global rankings:

### Most Notorious Potatoes

### Most Mysterious

### Most Connected

### Least Cooperative

### Potatoes To Watch

### Currently Missing

### Unreasonably Popular

### Longest Silence

### Most Frequently Upside Down

This creates the same social energy as the paid leaderboard example without requiring money.

People want to know:

> Why is Barbara famous?

And the answer may be:

Nobody entirely knows.

---

# Hidden achievements

The system should contain undisclosed conditions.

No public achievement checklist.

People discover them.

Examples:

**THE INVERTED ONE**  
Remain upside down during a thunderstorm.

**WITNESS**  
Be awake during a rare network event.

**THE LISTENER**  
Experience seven different types of music.

**OLD FRIEND**  
Maintain the same potato friendship for one year.

**NO COMMENT**  
Refuse to participate in five consecutive global votes.

**ABSOLUTE UNIT**  
Unknown.

The internet will inevitably begin documenting how these occur.

Some theories should be wrong.

# Human participation

Humans should occasionally become useful to the potatoes.

Examples:

> We require a number between 1 and 37.

---

> Please place me somewhere dark.

---

> I need to hear something loud.

---

The public website should extend the mystery rather than become a conventional game dashboard.

Possible sections:

8,411 asleep

**WORLD FEED**

# Admin / Control Room

Separate from the public site.

Possible sections:

### Population

See aggregate device state.

### Potato lookup

Inspect a potato's history for debugging.

### Event designer

Create scheduled and conditional events.

### Broadcast console

Send global or targeted messages.

### AI Writer's Room

Generate event ideas, dialogue and story arcs.

### World variables

Control temporary global conditions.

### Simulation

Preview how several personality types would respond before deploying an event.

### Emergency controls

Cancel an event.

Disable AI.

Push firmware notice.

Put every potato into a harmless default state.

God should have a circuit breaker.

---

# What AI should NOT do

Do not let an unrestricted model continuously run every potato.

That would be:

- expensive
- unpredictable
- difficult to debug
- difficult to maintain continuity
- potentially repetitive
- unnecessary

Most potato behavior can come from conventional rules and event systems.

AI is used selectively when language or interpretation benefits from it.

A potato might receive only a handful of genuinely AI-generated moments per day.

Those moments feel special precisely because everything is not generated.

---

# Basic technical shape

At the highest level:

```text
PHYSICAL POTATO
      ↓
Wi-Fi
      ↓
POTATO SERVER
      ↓
World State + Potato State + Events
      ↓
Optional AI generation
      ↓
Response
      ↓
PHYSICAL POTATO
```

The device is essentially a little client.

The server owns the civilization.

That means we can change much of the experience without repeatedly rewriting firmware.

The same backend can support:

- AMOLED potato
- e-paper potato
- website
- admin interface
- eventually other hardware

# First prototype

Do **not** try to build Potato Civilization first.

Build the smallest possible thing that can produce the feeling.

## Prototype 0.1

Two devices.

Two potatoes.

One server.

Each potato has:

- unique ID
- name
- tiny personality
- current mood/state
- memory of a few events
- internet connection

Server can:

- send a message to either potato
- send a message to both
- schedule an event
- record a response
- make one potato affect the other

Device can:

- display potato
- display text
- send touch/button response
- report basic sensor/environment state

That's enough.

---

# First magical demo

This is the test I would build toward.

Two devices sit next to one another.

Potato A is Bernard.

Potato B is Margaret.

Nothing is happening.

Bernard suddenly displays:

> I think Margaret is hiding something.

Margaret does nothing.

A minute later Margaret displays:

> Bernard talks too much.

Then both return to normal.

Later one receives:

> Margaret would like your opinion.

**TRUST HER**  
**DON'T**

Choose one.

Nothing obvious happens.

Twenty minutes later the other device reacts to the decision.

If that makes us laugh, we've found the core.

Everything else comes later.

# Development philosophy

We should continually ask:

### Does this make the potato seem more alive?

### Does this make the world seem larger?

### Does this produce a story someone might tell another person?

### Does this create curiosity rather than obligation?

### Is this funnier because it is unexplained?

If not, don't build it.

---

# Tone

Very important.

The writing should be dry.

Understated.

Never aggressively “cute.”

Avoid:

> Yay!!! Your potato is SO HAPPY! 🥔❤️

Prefer:

> This is acceptable.

The potatoes take their meaningless civilization extremely seriously.

That is where much of the comedy comes from.

---

# The central joke

Humans possess astonishing technology.

Global networking.

Artificial intelligence.

Environmental sensors.

Cloud infrastructure.

High-resolution AMOLED displays.

And we use all of it to discover that:

> Bernard no longer recognizes the authority of the Potato Council.

Perfect.

# What we don't know yet

And we should deliberately leave these unresolved for now:

- exact graphic style
- how much direct interaction the human gets
- how potato personality evolves
- whether AI operates locally or entirely server-side
- backend technology
- weather provider
- authentication
- how devices are provisioned onto Wi-Fi
- how potatoes discover one another
- whether exact location is ever stored
- how frequently devices contact the server
- e-paper update limitations
- battery behavior
- cost of AI calls
- public website structure
- whether users can create events
- whether there are seasons or persistent story arcs
- how fame is calculated
- whether potatoes can die

My instinct on the last one:

**Potatoes do not die.**

They may disappear.

They may retire.

They may leave.

They may become unavailable for comment.

But nobody opens a desk toy one morning to discover their potato died because they went on vacation.

---

# The actual experiment

The first goal is not to build a product.

It is to build **Bernard and Margaret**.

Give them enough identity to recognize one another.

Give us a tiny control room.

Let us inject strange events.

Let their physical environments affect them.

Let AI occasionally interpret what happens.

Then put both devices on the desk and live with them for a week.

We watch for the moment when one of us starts talking about them as though they made a decision themselves.

That is the threshold.

If we reach that, then we build the Potato Internet.
